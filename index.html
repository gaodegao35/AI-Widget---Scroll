Scrolling that knows where you're going

A scrollbar tells you where you are. It never tells you what is there. This is a scrollbar that reads the document, says what is at each position, takes you there when you describe it in your own words, and brings you back.

What the research found

  • The location is known, the place is notPeople ask an assistant where something is, then still hunt for it by hand.
  • Regenerating beats scrollingWhen the answer is far back in a chat, people ask again rather than scroll.
  • Markers that don't distinguishThree scrollbar marks that all say "generated image" help nobody.
  • Losing the place you leftAfter looking elsewhere, readers return to the general area, rarely the line.

What to do once you're in

  • Hover the railon the right edge — labels for what is at each position, and on a long document a preview of the twenty pages around your cursor.
  • Press /and describe what you want in your own words. "Elderly skipper" finds the old captain; no shared words required.
  • Press ⌘[after any jump to return to exactly where you were reading. Undo, for scrolling.
  • Hover a code block or imageand pin it. It stays on screen while you scroll to the thing you want to compare it against.
